For a class of Calabi--Yau hypersurfaces in toric fourfolds, we identify the integral relations among exceptional
curves of a small resolution with those among vanishing three-spheres in the Batyrev mirror family, and exclude
nonzero torsion combinations of the spheres. Near the maximally degenerate limit, every relation is realised by an
integral four-chain lifted from a tropical two-cycle, relative to the discriminant, on Gross's degeneration base.
The locus of mirror hypersurfaces with the prescribed number of nodes and no other singularities is smooth, with
local equations given by independent vanishing-cycle periods. After marking and orienting the nodes, the integral
comparison extends along every connected component of this locus meeting the near-limit construction, and the
bounding chains continue by transport. On each such component the codimension equals the relative Picard number,
establishing Morrison's predicted count there. In an explicit four-node family, we classify the entire
codimension-three locus as one such component; all other components have codimension four and no relation.
Every member of the former component has a projective small resolution, and for general members these resolutions
are birational to Batyrev mirrors of smoothings of the original nodal threefold. The comparison characterises smoothability and yields a symplectic
conifold transition on the mirror whenever a smoothing exists. The results apply to nodal crepant partial
resolutions, induced by projective toric morphisms, of anticanonical hypersurfaces whose singular points are nodes
and cones over del Pezzo surfaces of degrees six and seven, one on each singular two-face. The required unimodular
triangulations are verified by computer for the $51{,}827$ such polytopes identified in the Kreuzer--Skarke list.
