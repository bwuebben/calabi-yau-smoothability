\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Morrison proposed that mirror symmetry reverses conifold transitions of Calabi--Yau threefolds, exchanging
exceptional curves with vanishing three-spheres \cite{Mor99}. For the class of Batyrev hypersurfaces considered here,
we establish his numerical prediction and strengthen it to an equality of integral relation lattices. Every relation
is realised by an explicit integral bounding chain on the mirror hypersurface. The comparison also characterises
smoothability in terms of mirror vanishing cycles and yields a symplectic conifold transition whenever the original
nodal threefold is smoothable.

\subsection{Conifold transitions and Morrison's prediction}
Let $X'$ be a projective threefold with trivial canonical bundle whose singular points $q\in Q$ are ordinary double
points, and let $\widehat X\to X'$ be a projective small resolution, with exceptional curves $C_q\cong\mathbb P^1$. A smoothing
$X_s$ of $X'$, when it exists, contains a vanishing three-sphere for each node, and passing from $\widehat X$ to $X_s$
through $X'$ is a conifold transition.

In Morrison's picture, a mirror family of $\widehat X$ acquires nodes with vanishing spheres $\delta_q$ corresponding
to the curves $C_q$, and the nodal members admit small resolutions mirror to $X_s$. His numerical prediction is that
the $|Q|$ mirror nodes impose only $\rho_{\rm rel}$ conditions on the complex moduli, where $\rho_{\rm rel}$ is the
relative Picard number of $\widehat X\to X'$ \cite[\S3.1]{Mor99}. For the families below we prove this count and the
following integral comparison. Let $K\subset\Z^Q$ be the lattice of relations among the classes $[C_q]$ in
$H_2(\widehat X;\Z)$, of rank $|Q|-\rho_{\rm rel}$. On a suitable smooth member $Y$ of the mirror family,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-prediction}
\Bigl\{k\in\Z^Q:\ \sum_qk_q[\delta_q]=0\text{ in }H_3(Y;\Z)\Bigr\}=K,
\end{equation}
and the classes $[\delta_q]$ generate a torsion-free subgroup. We also show that the members on which all these
spheres collapse form a nonempty smooth locus of codimension $|Q|-\operatorname{rank}K=\rho_{\rm rel}$.
The integral equality specifies exactly which combinations of the mirror spheres are null-homologous.

This equality compares curves on $\widehat X$ with spheres on its mirror. Within a single conifold transition,
the relation spaces among exceptional curves and vanishing spheres are instead mutual annihilators
\cite[Thm.~1.14]{LLW18}, reflecting the topology studied by Clemens \cite{Cle83}.

Casta\~no-Bernard and Matessi gave a geometric construction of relations from tropical $2$-cycles with boundary on
the discriminant of an integral affine three-manifold \cite[Thm.~7.3, \S9.5]{CBM}. In their topological conifold models,
such a cycle produces chains bounded by the corresponding exceptional curves and vanishing spheres. Here we construct
integral bounding chains on the algebraic Batyrev mirror hypersurfaces and prove that they realise every relation.
We use \emph{relative tropical $2$-cycles}: integral two-chains on the affine base, with monodromy-invariant integral
tangent-vector coefficients, whose boundary follows the discriminant in its vanishing directions
(Definition~\ref{def:relcycle}). These extend the tropical cycles of \cite{CBM}. Their exhaustion of the relation
lattice and their lift to the mirror hypersurface are the geometric steps in the proof.

\subsection{The main theorem and its consequences}
Let $\Delta$ be an admissible reflexive four-polytope (Definition~\ref{def:admissible}) and
$X\subset\mathbb P_\Delta$ a $\Delta^\circ$-regular anticanonical hypersurface. Its singularities are nodes and isolated
cones over del Pezzo surfaces of degrees six and seven. A \emph{profile} $\sigma$ is a choice of subdivisions of its
singular two-faces into unit parallelograms and unimodular triangles (Definition~\ref{def:profile}). It determines a
nodal crepant partial resolution $X'_\sigma\to X$, with one node for each parallelogram \cite{Paper4,Paper5}.
We assume that this partial resolution is induced by a projective toric morphism, Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:P}.
The precise combinatorial restrictions are recalled below and in Section~\ref{sec:statements}.

Write $Q$ for the nodes. There is a projective small resolution $\widehat X\to X'_\sigma$, and the relation lattice
of its exceptional curves is computed by the parallelograms:
\[
K=\Bigl\{\lambda\in\Z^Q:\ \sum_q\lambda_q\,\circv_q=0\Bigr\}
\]
(Proposition~\ref{prop:integral}). Here $\circv_q=e_b+e_d-e_a-e_c$ is the \emph{circuit} of the corresponding
parallelogram $abcd$, with the sign fixed by the chosen diagonal, and $e_v$ is the formal basis vector indexed by
the lattice point $v$. On the mirror side, $Y_t$ is a member of the Batyrev family near its maximally degenerate
limit $t\to0$. The construction starts with real coefficients of a fixed sign pattern, close to the locus where
the face polynomial of each parallelogram factors into two binomials. For coefficients in the window specified
in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, there is one nondegenerate critical point with small nonzero critical value near each
parallelogram; its vanishing sphere is $\delta_q\subset Y_t$.

\begin{theorem*}[Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Let $\Delta$ be admissible and let $\sigma$ satisfy Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:P}. For $t$ sufficiently small and real
coefficients in the window of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\sum_qk_q[\delta_q]$ is a torsion class of $H_3(Y_t;\Z)$ only if $k\in K$, and it vanishes for $k\in K$. So the
relation lattice of the vanishing spheres is exactly $K$, and the $[\delta_q]$ span a free group $\Z^Q/K$ that meets the
torsion trivially.
\item Every relation $k\in K$ is the boundary of an explicit integral $4$-chain on $Y_t$ lifted from a relative
tropical $2$-cycle on the base of Gross's toric degeneration of $X'_\sigma$.
\item Near the given member, the members with a node at every one of these critical points form a nonempty complex
submanifold of codimension $|Q|-\operatorname{rank}K$, the relative Picard number of a projective small resolution
$\widehat X\to X'_\sigma$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem*}

\emph{Smoothings and symplectic transitions.} Friedman's relation criterion
\cite[Rem.~(4.5), Cor.~(4.7)]{Friedman}, in Namikawa's necessary and sufficient form \cite[Thm.~2.5]{Nam02},
characterises smoothability of $X'_\sigma$ by the existence of a relation among the exceptional curves with every
coefficient nonzero; compare \cite[Thm.~1.3]{RT09}. The integral comparison transfers this criterion to the mirror:
$X'_\sigma$ is smoothable if and only if the $[\delta_q]$ satisfy an integral relation with every coefficient nonzero.
In that case the spheres are smoothly isotopic to pairwise disjoint Lagrangian spheres for the restriction of a
toric K\"ahler form. By Smith--Thomas--Yau \cite[Thm.~2.9]{STY}, one of their $2^{|Q|}$ conifold transitions carries
a symplectic structure in which the exceptional curves are symplectic (Corollary~\ref{cor:smooth}). Thus algebraic
smoothability on the original side yields a symplectic conifold transition on the mirror.

The further geometric assertion in Morrison's proposal, that the nodal mirror members admit small resolutions
mirror to smoothings of $X'_\sigma$, remains open here.

\emph{A four-node example.} The polytope $\Delta_9$ of \cite{Paper5} has two unit squares and one
$dP_7$ pentagon, so $X'_\sigma$ has four nodes, and
$K=\Z\,(1,-1,-1,1)$. On the base $B$ of Gross's degeneration there is a disc whose boundary runs along the discriminant
through the four nodes along opposite legs, and whose node coefficients are $(1,-1,-1,1)$
(Section~\ref{ssec:x9}). Its lift is a $4$-chain on $Y_t$ bounded by $\delta_1-\delta_2-\delta_3+\delta_4$, and by the
theorem this is the only relation: the four spheres span a free group of rank three, and the members of the mirror family
with all four nodes form a locus of codimension three. Since $K$ contains a vector with no zero entry, $X'_\sigma$ is
smoothable, and the four spheres admit a symplectic conifold transition.

\subsection{Geometric realisation of the relations}
Let $B=B'_\sigma$ be the dual intersection complex of Gross's toric degeneration
of $X'_\sigma$ \cite{Gross2005}: an integral affine three-sphere whose singularities lie along a graph $\Gamma$, the
discriminant. Each node $q$ gives a four-valent vertex $p_q$ of $\Gamma$, which is a negative node in the sense of
\cite{CBM} (Proposition~\ref{prop:base}); the other vertices of $\Gamma$ are trivalent of the two standard types. Each arc
of $\Gamma$ carries a vanishing vector, the direction of the image of the monodromy minus the identity.

In the definition of relative tropical $2$-cycles, the coefficient sheaf is $\iota_*\Lambda$, where
$\Lambda$ is the local system of integral tangent vectors on $B\setminus\Gamma$ and $\iota$ is the inclusion.
Its sections across $\Gamma$ are monodromy-invariant. The boundary of a relative cycle is a
\emph{balanced network} on $\Gamma$, and at each node it has a \emph{node coefficient} $\rho_q\in\Z$, the jump of the
boundary coefficient across $q$ measured against the circuit $\circv_q$ (Definition~\ref{def:nodecoef}). The tropical
$2$-cycles of \cite{CBM} are examples; relative tropical $2$-cycles may in addition carry non-primitive fields, branch
arbitrarily, cross the discriminant with any invariant field, and pass a node in both directions. Their node
coefficients always lie in $K$ (Section~\ref{ssec:cycles-bdry}). The converse is the integral filling theorem:

\begin{theorem*}[Theorem~\ref{thm:real}]
Every balanced network on $\Gamma$ bounds a relative tropical $2$-cycle, and the node coefficients of balanced networks
are exactly $K$. Hence the node coefficients of relative tropical $2$-cycles generate $K$ over $\Z$.
\end{theorem*}

The mirror lift takes every relative tropical $2$-cycle $z$ to an integral $4$-chain $\check\Xi(z)$ on $Y_t$ with
\[
\partial\,\check\Xi(z)=\sum_q\rho_q(z)\,[\delta_q]
\]
(Theorem~\ref{thm:lift}). Together these two theorems construct a bounding chain for every $k\in K$ on the mirror
hypersurface. The period argument then proves that there are no further relations and no nonzero torsion
combinations of the spheres.

We call the tropical $2$-cycles $(S,j,v)$ of \cite[Def.~7.2]{CBM} \emph{strict}, to distinguish them from relative
cycles. Away from the discriminant, their mirror lifts are, up to homotopy in each region, the bundles of two-tori
annihilated by $v$ in the dual torus fibres, as in the topological construction of \cite{CBM}.
Remark~\ref{rem:strict-statement} specifies the comparison near the discriminant and the corrections needed to
complete the chains. On the resolution side we construct, for every such strict cycle with $j$ piecewise linear,
an integral $3$-chain
on $\widehat X$ with boundary $\sum_q\rho_q(S)[C_q]$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:res}). Thus the same tropical cycle constructs
a relation among the exceptional curves of the original threefold and among the vanishing spheres of the mirror.
Whether strict cycles already generate $K$ is Conjecture~\ref{conj:strict}; the evidence is discussed in
Section~\ref{ssec:evidence}. The integral exhaustion proved here uses relative cycles and addresses the
relation-generating aspect of \cite[Conj.~8.3]{CBM} for that enlarged class. It does not settle their full conjecture.

\subsection{Proof of the integral comparison}
The proof combines an integral filling theorem on the affine base, a geometric lift to the mirror, and a period
calculation. The first two steps realise all elements of $K$ as boundaries; the third establishes completeness and
determines the nodal codimension.

\emph{The base.} A circuit relation $\sum_q\lambda_q\circv_q=0$ is converted into a boundary problem on
$\partial\Delta\cong S^3$. Using $H_1(S^3)=0$, we construct a balanced network with node coefficients $\lambda$
(Section~\ref{ssec:cycles-bdry}). To fill it, we push the network off $\Gamma$ along the dual cells. The resulting
cycle in $B\setminus\Gamma$, with coefficients in $\Lambda$, must bound modulo meridians. A Mayer--Vietoris argument
shows that ordinary $H_1(B\setminus\Gamma;\Z)$ is generated by meridians; a computation on the boundary spheres of
the facets, using $H^1(S^2;\Z)=0$, controls the local coefficients. This integral complement argument is the main
step in the filling theorem (Section~\ref{sec:cycles}).

\emph{The lift.} The bases $B'_\sigma$ and $B_Y$ of the original and mirror degenerations have different cell
decompositions. A complex of pairs of dual cells gives a piecewise linear homeomorphism between them, matching the
discriminants and dualising the tangent local systems (Sections~\ref{sec:dualpair} and~\ref{sec:legendre}). We then
use a localized hypersurface $W^*$ in the sense of Mikhalkin and Yamamoto \cite{Mikhalkin,Ya16}, isotopic to $Y_t$.
Near each node it has the conifold smoothing model $\{xy+z_1z_2=\kappa\}$ with $\kappa$ small and nonzero
(Section~\ref{sec:regime}). The chain is assembled from thimbles at the nodes, pieces of toric divisors along its
boundary, and two-torus bundles over its interior. Each summand is lifted over its own domain, so crossings or
coincident sheets contribute separate chains. The local extensions leave a possible boundary in the class of the
fibre three-torus. This obstruction vanishes because the positive real locus meets each fibre torus once and misses
every vanishing sphere (Section~\ref{sec:lift}). The isotopy carries the resulting integral chain to $Y_t$.

\emph{Periods.} The chains give $K\subseteq\Rel(\delta)$. The periods
$\wp_q=\int_{\delta_q}\Omega$ extend holomorphically to the nodal members and equal their critical values
$\varepsilon_P$ times nonvanishing functions, where $P$ is the parallelogram of $q$. Up to nonzero scalars, the
differentials of the critical values are close to the linear forms defined by the circuits. The period differentials have rank
$|Q|-\operatorname{rank}K$, which bounds the rank of $\Rel(\delta)$ from above by $\operatorname{rank}K$.
Since $K$ is saturated, the inclusion is an equality; the same argument excludes nonzero torsion combinations of
the spheres. Critical-value equations for a basis of the circuit span meet transversally, and the remaining
critical values vanish on their intersection. This gives the smooth nodal locus and its codimension
(Section~\ref{sec:periods}). The period argument is classical \cite{GMS95,LLW18}; here it is made uniform near the
maximally degenerate limit and combined with the integral chains.

\subsection{Hypotheses}
Admissibility means that the edges of $\Delta$ are primitive and its two-faces are unimodular triangles, unit squares,
or lattice pentagons and hexagons with one interior lattice point and no boundary lattice points besides their
vertices. The squares give nodes, and the pentagons and hexagons give cones over del Pezzo surfaces of degrees seven
and six. The edge of the polar polytope $\Delta^\circ$ dual to each non-triangular two-face is required to have lattice
length one, so that each such face contributes exactly one singular point. A profile specifies the subdivisions of
these faces into unit parallelograms and unimodular triangles; the choices occur at the hexagons.

For the polytope and profile, Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:P} is the only restriction beyond admissibility. It is needed for
the projective partial resolution and Gross degeneration used here, and is a genuine restriction. In the test set of
$612$ admissible polytopes of Section~\ref{ssec:list}, it fails for $11$ of the $375$ hexagon-free polytopes with
$K\neq0$ and for every profile on $59$ of the $142$ polytopes with a hexagon (Section~\ref{ssec:scope}).

The initial mirror construction uses the real coefficient window of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}; the resulting relations
extend to neighbouring smooth complex members as explained in Section~\ref{ssec:relations}. Near the maximally
degenerate limit the nodal locus lies close to, but in general not on, the locus where the face polynomials factor.
The terms from the cells next to each parallelogram move the critical values
(Section~\ref{ssec:periodsremarks}).

Two further conditions enter the construction and hold automatically. The base requires a regular unimodular
triangulation of $\partial\Delta^\circ$ with a height
satisfying Gross's convexity conditions; Theorem~\ref{thm:U} shows that one exists for every admissible polytope, by
a computer-assisted verification over the $51{,}827$ admissible polytopes of the Kreuzer--Skarke classification,
with each triangulation and its height verified in exact arithmetic (Appendix~\ref{app:unimodular}). This larger computation
is distinct from the test set of $612$ polytopes used for the other statistics. The mirror side requires a subdivision of $\partial\Delta$
in which the cells next to each node parallelogram have their other vertices at lattice height one; the pulling
refinement of the subdivision given by~\ref{hyp:P} has this property (Proposition~\ref{prop:pulling}).

\subsection{Relation to other work}
Batyrev and Kreuzer studied conifold transitions for Batyrev hypersurfaces whose singular two-faces are unit squares
\cite{BK10}. They identified the curve classes with the circuits of the squares and specialised the mirror family to
the locus where each square's face polynomial factors into two binomials. They observed that a projective, hence
symplectic, small resolution of such a degenerate mirror requires a relation among the vanishing spheres with every
coefficient nonzero, but did not determine whether such a relation exists \cite[\S3]{BK10}.
Corollary~\ref{cor:smooth} answers the analogous question for the nodal members constructed here: the relation exists
exactly when $X'_\sigma$ is smoothable. As noted above, these members approach the factorisation locus as $t\to0$.

Casta\~no-Bernard and Matessi constructed Lagrangian torus fibrations over integral affine three-manifolds with
singularities \cite{CBM09} and the topological conifolds and cycles described above \cite{CBM}. Their
Conjecture~8.3 relates tropical cycles, homological relations, and tropical resolutions and smoothings; their result
producing tropical cycles from vanishing-cycle relations in a family of compact models is conditional on a basis
conjecture \cite[Prop.~9.2]{CBM}. The present paper proves integral exhaustion by relative cycles on Gross's bases
and constructs their bounding chains on the Batyrev mirror hypersurfaces.

Gross and Siebert developed the correspondence between toric degenerations and their affine data, with the discrete
Legendre transform that exchanges the two sides of the mirror \cite{GSI}; Haase and Zharkov
constructed the integral affine spheres of toric hypersurfaces and their torus fibrations \cite{HZ1}. Cycles in
Calabi--Yau hypersurfaces near a maximally degenerate limit have been built from tropical data in other settings:
Lagrangian rational homology spheres from tropical curves with ends on the discriminant by Mak and Ruddat
\cite{MR20}, and lifts of closed tropical $(p,q)$-cycles in hypersurfaces with unimodular tropicalisation by
Yamamoto \cite{Ya26}, who notes that the tropical $2$-cycles of \cite{CBM} can be regarded as tropical $(1,2)$-cycles
\cite[\S1, footnote]{Ya26}. Ruddat and Siebert compute period integrals over lifts of tropical one-cycles \cite{RS20},
Ruddat develops a homology theory of tropical cycles on integral affine manifolds with a perfect pairing \cite{Ru21},
and Abouzaid, Ganatra, Iritani and Sheridan compute periods tropically in the setting of the Gamma conjecture
\cite{AGIS20}. The chains constructed here differ from these in two respects: they are relative, with boundary at the
nodes, and the subdivision that defines the ambient toric variety of $X'_\sigma$ is not unimodular, since it contains the
node parallelograms. The period argument of Section~\ref{sec:periods} goes back to Greene, Morrison and Strominger
\cite{GMS95}; compare \cite{LLW18}.

\subsection{Organisation}
Section~\ref{sec:statements} states the precise hypotheses and results. Part~I constructs the base and its cycles:
Section~\ref{sec:base} describes the partial resolution and Gross's base, Section~\ref{sec:dualpair} constructs the
complex of pairs of dual cells, and Section~\ref{sec:cycles} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:real}. In Part~II,
Section~\ref{sec:legendre} transfers cycles to the mirror base, Section~\ref{sec:regime} constructs the localized
mirror and Morse charts, Section~\ref{sec:lift} lifts the cycles (Theorem~\ref{thm:lift}), and
Section~\ref{sec:periods} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:smooth}. Part~III,
Section~\ref{sec:resolution}, gives the resolution-side chains (Theorem~\ref{thm:res}), independently of Parts~I
and~II. Part~IV, Section~\ref{sec:examples}, treats examples and the evidence for Conjecture~\ref{conj:strict}.
Appendix~\ref{app:unimodular} proves the triangulation theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:U}; the remaining appendices
contain deferred proofs and data.
